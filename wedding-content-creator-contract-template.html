Most wedding content creator disputes do not come from dishonest people. They come from two excited parties who assumed different things: when the reels arrive, who is allowed to post them, and what happens to the raw clips.

A written agreement does not make your business feel corporate. It removes the awkward conversations before the champagne starts flowing, so everyone can relax. Below is why each clause exists, then a free fill-in template you can copy today.

The three clauses that cause the most fights

Across wedding forums and creator communities, the same three promises appear in almost every complaint. Get these right and you prevent the majority of disputes.

01 Turnaround

"You will have it tomorrow" is not a deadline. Write the exact window (for example, edited reels within 48 hours), how delivery happens, and what you owe if you miss it.

02 Usage rights

Who owns the footage, where each side may post it, whether the creator can use it in a portfolio or paid ads, and whether the couple can be tagged or named.

03 Raw footage

How many clips, hosted where (Google Drive, a gallery), for how many days the link stays live, and whether the creator keeps a backup after delivery.

The 12 clauses your agreement needs

Use these whether you are a creator writing your own package or a couple checking the contract you were sent. Each one links to the exact blank in the template below.

Clause 01

Parties and event details

The full legal names of the couple (or the person paying), the creator's business name and address, the venue, and the exact date. If a planner or agency is paying, state who is actually a party to the contract.

Fill in: names, business entity, venue address, wedding date, and the person authorized to approve changes.
Clause 02

Scope of coverage

The start and end time, number of hours, which parts of the day are covered (getting ready, ceremony, reception, send-off), and what is excluded. State the primary tool (phone) so no one expects cinema-grade footage.

Fill in: coverage window, number of hours, named moments included, and gear.
Clause 03

Deliverables and edits

Specify the number of finished short-form videos, their length, aspect ratio (9:16 vertical), and whether they are cut to trending audio. Then state the raw footage: an estimate of clips, format, and where the gallery lives.

Fill in: "___ edited reels, ___ to ___ seconds each, vertical 9:16, plus the full raw gallery."
Clause 04

Turnaround and delivery method

The single most important clause. Name the exact deadline for the first edited reel and for the raw gallery, the delivery platform, and the remedy for a missed deadline (a partial refund, an extension, or a credit).

Fill in: "First edited reel within ___ hours of coverage; full gallery within ___ hours, delivered by ___."
Clause 05

Usage rights and licensing

Grant the couple a personal, non-commercial license to post and share, while letting the creator use clips for portfolio and self-promotion unless the couple opts out in writing. Address paid ads separately, since that is a commercial use.

Fill in: allowed platforms, portfolio permission (yes/no), paid-ad permission (yes/no), and whether faces or names may be shown.
Clause 06

Payment terms

Total fee, non-refundable retainer to hold the date, when the balance is due, accepted payment methods, and the fee for late payment. A typical structure is a 25 to 50 percent retainer with the balance due before the wedding.

Fill in: total $___, retainer $___, balance due date, overtime rate $___ per hour.
Clause 07

Cancellation, rescheduling, and force majeure

What happens to the retainer if the couple cancels, if the creator cannot attend (a refund and help finding a replacement, or a refund only), and how a date change is handled. Include illness, weather, and other events outside either side's control.

Fill in: cancellation refund ladder, reschedule rules, and creator's backup obligation.
Clause 08

Overtime, travel, and extra costs

Define when overtime starts and its hourly rate, mileage or travel fees, parking, and whether a vendor meal is provided for coverage over a certain number of hours. Couples repeatedly ask about the meal on wedding forums, so write it down.

Fill in: overtime $___/hour, travel beyond ___ miles, parking, vendor meal yes/no.
Clause 09

Second shooter and assistants

State whether anyone works alongside the lead creator, whether that changes the price, and that the lead remains responsible for delivery. This avoids a surprise stranger showing up with a second phone.

Fill in: second shooter yes/no, included or added fee, and who owns the extra footage.
Clause 10

Vendor coordination and priority

The creator agrees not to obstruct the contracted photographer or videographer and to follow their cue during key moments. Some photo and video contracts claim priority over any other camera operator, and a growing number name content creators explicitly. Check those contracts first.

Fill in: who leads during ceremony/formal moments, and a promise not to interfere with other vendors.
Clause 11

Liability and footage failure

Cap the creator's liability at the amount paid (standard for a one-person vendor), and describe what happens if footage is lost to equipment failure: a refund, possibly partial. Creators should record to two devices where possible.

Fill in: liability cap, refund terms for total or partial loss, and any equipment safeguards promised.
Clause 12

Disputes, governing law, and signatures

Name the state whose law applies, a good-faith negotiation period before any claim, and whether disputes go to mediation, arbitration, or small-claims court. End with dated signature lines for both sides.

Fill in: governing state, dispute path, and signature/date lines.

Frequently asked questions

Is this contract template legally binding?

A signed agreement that clearly states an offer, acceptance, payment, and scope is generally enforceable in most U.S. states, but requirements vary and this template is not legal advice. Have a local lawyer review it before you rely on it.

Can couples use this template too?

Yes. Use the 12 clauses as a checklist of what to ask a content creator before booking, and compare them against the agreement the creator sends. Watch clauses 4, 5, and 10 especially closely.

Is the template actually free?

Yes. It is a plain fill-in document you can copy into Google Docs, edit, and export as PDF. The email field exists only to send you the link and occasional creator notes.

What if my photographer contract forbids a content creator?

Raise it in writing before booking a third vendor. Some photo and video contracts restrict additional camera operators, and a growing number name content creators explicitly. Clause 10 of the template addresses on-day coordination but cannot override an earlier signed contract.

What goes wrong without a written contract?

Most disputes come down to three missing promises: the exact turnaround window, who may post and where, and how raw footage is delivered. A verbal "you will have it tomorrow" is hard to enforce once something goes wrong.
